**Solo Wedding Project: Bird (Film Full Photo) Captures Self-Love with the Lomomatic 110**
Fotógrafos: aomschll

For Bird, the photographer behind Film Full Photo, shooting on film is more than about documenting a moment; it's a way of telling stories through the photographer's own eye and emotions. She used the Lomomatic 110 to bring her Solo Wedding concept to life, a personal project she created to share the idea that “Happiness doesn't depend on someone else – it begins with ourselves.” Every frame captured with this tiny camera not only preserves a meaningful moment, but also reflects her unique creative vision and heartfelt approach to storytelling. If you're looking for inspiration for your next shoot, read on and discover Bird's beautiful images captured with the Lomomatic 110. Solo Wedding Project by Film Full Photo Welcome to the Magazine, Bird! How did you first get into photography, and what was your first camera? My journey began when I was in vocational school studying design. Photography was offered as an elective, and that's where I first picked up a Nikon FM2. Developing and printing photos in the darkroom with my classmates was a lot of fun, but at the time I wasn't especially drawn to photography, because my dream back then was to become an interior designer, so I was much more interested in drawing perspectives and painting with watercolors. Still, the Nikon FM2 was the very first camera I ever used, and it marked the beginning of my analogue journey. - - - Credits: Film Full Photo ,  Shot with the Lomomatic 110 & Tiger 110 ISO 200 ,  Model: @cartuneishappyverygoodgirl What was your first impression of the Lomomatic 110? “How can something this cute exist?” That was honestly my first thought when I saw it and held it in my hands. Even the film cartridge looked adorable. Everything about it felt playful and reminded me of LEGO toys from when I was a kid. I have to admit that the first roll was a bit challenging because I wasn't used to zone focusing. But by the second and third rolls, I was having so much fun. I found that shooting this way let me react much more quickly and capture the exact moments I wanted. - - - Credits: Film Full Photo ,  Shot with the Lomomatic 110 & Tiger 110 ISO 200 ,  Model: @cartuneishappyverygoodgirl Please tell us about your concept for this shoot. Solo Wedding started as a fun personal idea inspired by a photography trend in Japan, where many women choose to wear wedding dresses and have bridal portraits taken without a groom. To me, it represents the joy and freedom of living life on your own terms. Whether you're in a relationship or not, whether you choose to get married or not, you can still wear a beautiful wedding dress and celebrate yourself through photography. I started this project by photographing myself wearing a wedding dress. I wore a vintage dress from my own shop and captured a series of self-portraits in front of the dressing room mirror. - - Solo Wedding Project by Film Full Photo In your photos with the Lomomatic 110, the bride is seen enjoying the beach on her own without a groom. Can you expound on the particular message you wanted to convey? I've noticed that whenever people see beautiful pre-wedding photos, many women leave comments like, “I wish I could do a photoshoot like this, but I don't have a boyfriend,” or “If I ever get married, I have to shoot with this photographer.” That made me wonder, what if you didn't have to wait for someone else to be there? What if you could simply do it for yourself? That question became the starting point of this project. I decided to turn the idea into reality, and in the end, I truly loved how it turned out. I wanted to incorporate something that genuinely reflected who she is, so blending her interests into the shoot felt like the right approach. The location – Tal Sam Ton Beach in Pranburi – has a beautiful coastline and a long road running alongside the beach, making it the perfect setting. One of my favorite moments was watching Toon effortlessly hop onto her surf skate and ride along the shore. When people are free to be themselves, everything naturally falls into place. Credits: Film Full Photo ,  Shot with the Lomomatic 110 & Tiger 110 ISO 200 ,  Model: @cartuneishappyverygoodgirl If you had to choose the best time of day to shoot a pre-wedding session on film, what would it be? Film works beautifully at almost any time of day. For pre-wedding shoots, though, I always prioritize what works best for the couple. Most people prefer to sleep in and arrive well-rested, so I usually plan the session for the afternoon and continue shooting through sunset and into twilight. Finally, is there anything you'd like to share about your upcoming projects? I'd love for everyone to keep following the Solo Wedding project. I'll continue developing it with new themes and concepts so that each series feels fresh and different. I hope you'll look forward to the next set of images. Thank you so much! **First Impressions: Tree's Thailand Travel Snapshots with the Half-frame Simple Use Reloadable Film Camera**
Fotógrafos: jluo

We recently handed the new Half-frame Simple Use Reloadable Film Camera Classicolor to film shutterbug Tree, inviting her to document her travels across Thailand. The half‑frame format gave her room to shoot freely – sunrise on Koh Samet, street corners in Bangkok; all those fleeting, unplanned scenes. After her trip, we talked about the journey, the camera, and how film keeps memories fresh. Photo by Tree ,  Shot with the Half-frame Simple Use Reloadable Film Camera Classicolor Hey Tree, welcome back to Lomography Magazine! You took the new Half-frame Simple Use Reloadable Film Camera Classicolor with you to Thailand. How did the shooting go overall? Any unforgettable or funny moments you'd like to share with our readers? Shooting this roll felt light and easy. With a non-half‑frame camera, I tend to be more careful about when I press the shutter – I’d hesitate, wondering if a scene is worth the frame, or if I should save it for something else. But the half‑frame camera kind of sets you free. Double the shots, half the hesitation. It gives you this liberating sense that you can afford to waste a few. So this roll ended up full of light, casual clicks that I wouldn't normally have taken. The highlight was definitely on Koh Samet, watching the sunrise and then just wandering along the beach, sitting, spacing out, watching the waves roll in. I took a lot of photos that morning, pointing the camera wherever the sunlight hit: up at the sky, down at the rocks, random directions. At the time, I thought maybe I was overdoing it, but when the film came back, I was glad I did. Each frame carried the warmth of that moment, like being pulled back to the beach, with the light shimmering on the water and the sound of the tide still in my ears. - - Photos by Tree ,  Shot with the Half-frame Simple Use Reloadable Film Camera Classicolor You're no stranger to LomoChrome Classicolor 35 mm ISO 200, and you've shot half-frame cameras before, too. So, with these two coming together this time, did they end up surprising you in any unexpected ways? The colors of LomoChrome Classicolor are never too loud. They carry a certain cinematic quality, leaning warm and subdued. When paired with the half‑frame format this time, the zone‑focus lens seemed to make the grain more pronounced, giving the images a rough texture, almost like the grain of a fading memory. With Thailand’s intense sunlight and vivid surroundings, did the colors and atmosphere captured on film match the Thailand you remember? It does, though perhaps a little softer than my memory. In my mind, Thailand is vivid – bright, saturated, with that relentless tropical sunlight. But film tends to pull that intensity back slightly. Seeing the scans, I appreciated that distance. It didn’t amplify the heat; instead, it preserved the dampness of the sea breeze, the glare of the streets, with a touch of the dreamlike. For me, film doesn’t just record color. It holds onto the sensory texture of a moment as well. - - Photos by Tree ,  Shot with the Half-frame Simple Use Reloadable Film Camera Classicolor How did you find it as a travel companion, or as a camera you could simply pick up and take with you?
