1. **Leer** el texto completo de cada artículo a continuación.
2. **Resumir** cada artículo en 2-3 frases en español, destacando la inspiración fotográfica.
3. **Redactar un texto locutable** en español, con tono natural y cercano, como para un programa de radio.
4. **Generar el audio** del texto locutable: solo voz, sin música, sin efectos de sonido.

  - Un texto en español con los resúmenes de todos los artículos del día.
  - Un archivo de audio con la locución de ese texto.

A continuación tienes el texto completo de cada artículo. Léelos todos
y a partir de ahí genera el resumen locutable. No te saltes ningún artículo.

**Collecting Mailbox Memories: Stories from the Lomographers’ Print Exchange**
Fotógrafos: @agafa, agafa, @laracakmak, laracakmak, @agostc, @amy\_bellezza, @anttihoo, @atzipinki, @barker\_days\_ahead, @carnivalnoon, @crismiranda, @cul-de-sac, @eara, @elianarchivist, @elsvanopstal, @evelynedierikx, @frenchyfyl, @akio\_nakai, @fabio\_corsatto, @shoao, @smwh\_in, @gunnisonlee, @klubttu, @wil6ka, @ketchfoto, @mackiechartres, agostc, francinegaebriele

The Community has brought back one of our most cherished analogue traditions: the Lomographers’ Print Exchange. Since then, we have constantly been reminded of the simple yet magical act of sending and receiving photographs to connect one film lover to another. Over the past months, prints have crossed borders across the globe – from Brazil to Australia, Hong Kong to Spain, Poland to the United States, and more. We caught up with a few participants to see how their exchanges went down, inviting them to look back on the prints they sent and the postcards that have since found their way into their mailboxes. ## @agafa from Latvia: Personalized Prints For Each Recipient What I Sent After I received replies from other Lomographers, I started choosing which photos to print. I decided to make each postcard unique, so every participant received a different image that I felt matched their style and interests. I chose some of my favorite shots, including vibrant street scenes and colorful everyday moments. In total, I sent around 15 to 20 postcards to Lomographers in Japan, the USA, Australia, Spain, Finland, Romania, Belarus, France, Chile, and Brazil. The whole process was incredibly exciting. Before selecting a print, I carefully explored each person's LomoHome to find a photo that I thought they would truly enjoy. It made the exchange feel much more personal, and I loved the idea that every postcard was chosen especially for its recipient. - - Credits: agafa What I Received The first postcard I received was from Japan. I was absolutely thrilled! From that moment on, I checked my mailbox every single day because I couldn't wait to see what would arrive next. It felt like opening a surprise gift or an Advent calendar before Christmas. Seeing these prints in person is a completely different experience from viewing photos online. You can appreciate the paper, the colors, and the personal touch that each sender put into their postcard. To keep them safe, I bought a photo album where I now carefully store my growing collection. Credits: agafa ## @laracakmak from Spain: Sharing a Piece of Barcelona What I Sent I chose to send a photo of Somorrostro Beach in Barcelona, featuring El Peix, Frank Gehry’s iconic fish sculpture. I love the way the sunlight reflects off its metal scales, making it sparkle throughout the day. The sculpture was created for the 1992 Olympic Games, a time that completely transformed this part of the city. I was a child then, and I still remember visiting the area with my school to watch the construction progress. What had once been an industrial port became a vibrant waterfront welcoming athletes from around the world. Back then, Barcelona’s slogan was “Barcelona, posa’t guapa” (“Barcelona, make yourself beautiful”), and that spirit of renewal has always stayed with me. I wanted to share a place that represents both my city and my childhood memories. Credits: laracakmak What I Received Receiving postcards from Lomographers around the world has been one of my favorite experiences this year. As a child, my friends and I always exchanged postcards when we traveled, and I’ve missed that tradition ever since. Every postcard I received felt personal, showcasing a unique technique and a place or subject that meant something to its creator. Some even shared memories of visiting my city. A few postcards were lost on both sides along the way, but that’s part of the adventure. Thanks to my dear Lomographers: @agostc, @amybellezza, @anttihoo, @atzipinki, @barkerdaysahead, @carnivalnoon, @crismiranda, @cul-de-sac, @eara, @elianarchivist, @elsvanopstal, @evelynedierikx, @frenchyfyl, and @akionakai. I’m especially grateful to @evelynedierikx for the photo booklet, prints, and postcards. I love the theme of angels, so her work was particularly meaningful to me. I keep all of them together in my journal, as a collection of memories and inspiration. Credits: laracakmak ## @fabiocorsatto from Italy: Snaps of Landscapes and Feline Friends What I Sent I usually photograph portraits, but for this exchange, I chose to send landscapes captured during short trips and a few pictures of my cats – these are photos that I normally don’t post on any social media platform except the Lomography website. There wasn’t any particular reason behind this choice. They were simply images I liked and felt like sharing. So far, I’ve exchanged postcards with Akio ( @akionakai) from Japan, João ( @shoao) from Brazil, JL ( @smwhin) from Germany, Gunnison ( @gunnisonlee) from the United States, Philippe ( @frenchyfyl) from France, Igor ( @klubttu) from the United Kingdom, and Laura ( @eara) from Austria. I still keep sending postcards to this day. Sometimes they get lost along the way or are returned to me even though the address is correct, but that doesn’t discourage me. I just resend them. After all, this too is part of the beauty of a photographic exchange through the mail. - - - - - - - - - Credits: @fabiocorsatto What I Received Working in a photography shop and taking care of other people’s photographs is part of my daily routine. Receiving these postcards, however, was something completely different. What I enjoyed the most was waiting – not knowing what would arrive, who had chosen that particular photograph, or when it would be delivered to me. Each postcard was a little surprise, and I always felt joy and warmth knowing that somewhere in the world, someone had thought of me and decided to send me that photograph. Each postcard traveled from one country to another before reaching my hands, and that makes them even more special. I keep all the postcards in a box at the shop where I work, and from time to time, I like to show them to anyone who comes in. I want to thank the people that allowed me to participate in this project and those that exchanged this experience with me! Credits: @fabiocorsatto ## @shoao from Brazil: Special Envelopes of Photos and Stories What I Sent I selected 40 photos and printed them on silk-finish photographic paper. Everyone I exchanged prints with received a unique print. I also tried to include something meaningful with each one – a behind-the-scenes story or a bit of context about where and how the photograph was made. Inside each envelope, I added a few smaller prints from my early analogue years and, in some cases, a piece from an old project of mine called Postcards from the End of the World. I sent prints to Italy, Germany, the Czech Republic, Canada, the United States, Greece, France, Brazil, the United Kingdom, Austria, Finland, New Zealand, Argentina, Spain, Chile, Romania, and Georgia. Isn't it beautiful how far an idea can travel? - - - - - Credits: @wil6ka, @ketchfoto, & @mackiechartres (sent by @shoao) What I Received The first postcard I received was from @fabiocorsatto. There’s something special about receiving a letter with a printed photo and a handwritten message from another country. That's something we should never lose. After the first one, more kept arriving, and the feeling was always the same. This community is amazing. Keep sending postcards. Letters. Anything. We could all use a little more of that. Credits: @shoao ## @agostc from Argentina: Sending Travel Souvenirs to the World What I Sent I sent some photos I took during trips to different places, mostly of rooms or landscapes that gave me a sense of being somewhere else in the world. I felt like sharing that feeling of being in another part of the globe because it's similar to sending a postcard: a small part of you travels to an unexpected place. That's how I ended up sending photos to people living in Ireland, Italy, the United States, the Philippines, Georgia, Estonia, Belarus, Spain, and Argentina. - - - - - - - Credits: agostc What I Received Every time I knew someone had sent me a postcard, I checked my mailbox every day until it arrived. I get that same feeling whenever I take a roll of film to the photo lab. That's why shooting on film, printing photos, and sending postcards feel so special to me. It's completely different from the fast-paced, instant nature of the digital world. I also love the way you connect with other Lomography members by expressing your gratitude for each exchange. That's why I keep all the postcards in a special box, together with other meaningful postcards I've received from my family or for my birthday. Credits: agostc It’s never too late to join the fun! **Recap: Birmingham Design Festival LomoWalk**

Birmingham Design Festival was created by designers Daniel Alcorn and Luke Tonge and is an annual celebration of local, national and international designers who come together for a packed program of talks, workshops and events from some of the most innovative people in the industry. This year, we brought a bit of Lomography magic to Birmingham with a special LomoApparat LomoWalk around the graffiti-clad streets of Digbeth using some LomoChrome Purple 35 mm ISO 100-400 and LomoChrome Turquoise 35 mm film, and a whole lot of Kaleidoscope, multiple exposure, and Splitzer experimentation. - - - - Photos by Hannah Brown ,  Taken on the Lomo MC-A In true Birmingham-style, the day started out rainy and grey, and switched to glorious sunshine as soon as we stepped outside. We set up this walk in Digbeth, Birmingham's very own creative quarter. It's an old industrial area that has been appropriated by artists and creatives who make the most of the Victorian factory building to set up art studios and offices. - - - - Photos by Lauren Skillman ,  Shot with the LomoApparat & LomoChrome Purple 35 mm ISO 100-400 There are some impressive graffiti murals dotted around in the most unexpected corners of Digbeth and, as you can see form the photos results, our participants really set to work photographing as much as they could. We talked to one of the participants, Lauren Skillman, about her experience of this LomoWalk. - - - - - - Craig Smith, Michela Dunn and friends of John Hall ,  Shot with the LomoApparat & LomoChrome Purple 35 mm ISO 100-400 Hello Lauren, please tell us why you signed up to this walk. I’ve been on lots of group photo walks and photomarathons before, trying various kinds of cameras wherever possible. Lomography was new to me, and I was keen to experiment with the interesting color filters and gritty textures it can create. It was nice to do a practical workshop in addition to seeing the talks at Birmingham Design Festival, and events like this are a great chance to meet people with similar creative interests. - - - - Splitzer and Kaleidoscope madness by XX ,  Shot with the LomoApparat and the Lomography Color Negative 35 mm ISO 400 How did you find the experience and shooting with the LomoApparat? The LomoApparat was similar to a [point-and-shoot] camera but with extra features, and we were also given lenses to experiment with. It took a moment to get used to not being able to see the effects through the viewfinder. After some practice, I gained intuition in what was worth taking a photo of (there were only 36 opportunities on the film reel), and which subjects would work well together when layered on top of each other via double exposure. - - - - - - Charlotte Powell, John Hall and Margot Edwards ,  Shot with the LomoApparat and the LomoChrome Turquoise 35 mm film How did you find the results, and do you think you would try shooting film again? Around 1/3 of the images came out as hoped. The kaleidoscope lens created fun fragmented shots and was most effective when there was a solid and vibrant subject like the street art/graffiti around Digbeth. The camera was a bit limited without a zoom or focus, so some images came out blurry. And of course, I got the classic accidental finger in shot! I didn’t put any pressure on myself to get ‘perfect’ shots though because it was my first time doing Lomography, but I would definitely work with film again to develop my experience with the craft. That said, my visual style is quite abstract and surreal, so I liked being pleasantly surprised by the quirky outcomes! A huge thank you to everyone who took part and made this event such a success!
